Crankcase breather → catch can
Reliability59X applies
Routes crankcase blow-by to a 100ml catch can instead of the airbox. Keeps the filter dry, carb clean, and removes oily deposits from the intake. Blank off the airbox breather inlet. Empty the can at every oil change.
Effort:
5th gear — never lug below 4,000–4,500 rpm
ReliabilityFree
The pre-3TB gearbox has a known 5th gear lubrication weakness at low rpm. Keep 5th above 4,500 rpm and the gear pair is reliably lubricated. Forum members report 50,000+ km trouble-free with this discipline alone.
Effort:
KEDO oil temperature dipstick
Reliability
Replaces the oil filler cap with a bimetallic temperature gauge. Critical for an air-cooled single in summer or slow traffic. KEDO part 91037, fits XT500/550/600/660. Verify thread on the 59X oil tank before ordering — the front-triangle oil tank location is non-standard.~€44 from KEDO.de

Synthetic 10W-40 motorcycle oil
ReliabilityEasy win
Eliminates the hot neutral-finding difficulty reported on mineral oil. Wet-clutch compatible required. Elf Moto 4 ECO 10W-40 or Motul 5100 10W-40 widely available in France. Change at 3,000km on a fresh rebuild, then 4–5,000km thereafter.
Effort:
Chain sprocket guard / engine bash plate
Reliability
A 3mm steel plate in front of the countershaft sprocket prevents a snapped chain from punching through the engine cases. A dropped chain at speed destroys an unprotected casing instantly. 30-minute fabrication from flat stock. Also protects sump and oil filter on rough terrain.
Effort:
Oil cooler — KEDO kit or generic XT600 unit
ReliabilityPerformance59X applies
The most cited niche reliability mod on the XT/TT family. The 59X has no factory oil cooler. KEDO billet aluminium kit taps into the secondary oil circuit with no case drilling. Alternatively, a used XT600 cooler (~€25) with fabricated braided lines. Reduces oil temperature, preserves viscosity, directly reduces crankshaft bearing and 5th gear wear.KEDO kit ~€150–180 (check stock) / DIY ~€50–80

Needle clip raised one position (richer mid-range)
ReliabilityPerformance59X applies
The YDIS carb is factory-set lean to pass emissions. Moving the needle clip one position toward the pointy end richens the mid-range and addresses the flat spot at 1/4–1/2 throttle. On the YDIS needle verify if clip grooves exist (use shims if not — 2 nylon shims from a jet kit).Free / €5–10 for shim kit

Inline fuel filter
ReliabilityEasy win
A transparent inline fuel filter between petcock and carb catches debris and lets you monitor fuel cleanliness. Remove the tiny stock float needle filter at the same time — it restricts flow and the inline filter replaces it.~€3–5

FCR flatslide carburettor swap
Performance
Keihin FCR 40mm flatslide eliminates YDIS complexity and dramatically improves throttle response. Starting point (FCR41, vented airbox, FMF exhaust): 155 main jet, 45 pilot, NCVT needle. Requires full jetting to suit your setup. Not for a road-registered bike seeking simplicity.€200–400 used
Effort:
1VJ Ténéré airbox conversion (open intake)
Performance
The 1VJ airbox with both original intakes sealed and the rear cap open improves top-end breathing noticeably. Requires richer main jetting to compensate. Less impactful on the 59X which already has a high-flow race airbox from factory.

Progressive fork springs (Hyperpro / Progressive Suspension)
PerformanceReliability
Stock 59X springs dive heavily under braking on the road. Hyperpro makes progressive springs for the XT600 family (verify 43mm fitment). Pair with 15W fork oil. Progressive springs have more volume than stock — set oil level by volume (440ml/leg), not by old oil measurement.
Effort:
15W fork oil + 1–2 psi air pressure
PerformanceEasy win
Switching from stock 10W to 15W noticeably reduces brake dive. Adding 1–2 psi via the fork top cap increases effective spring rate for heavier riders or loaded riding. Fully reversible, costs nothing beyond the oil.
Effort:
YSS or Wilbers rear shock replacement
PerformanceReliability
If the stock Monocross shock body is beyond rebuild, YSS and Wilbers make direct replacement units for the 43F/1VJ/3AJ family. Öhlins historically made one too (rare). Avoid Hyperpro spring-only on the old shock body — multiple forum reports of breakage.

Phosphor bronze suspension linkage bushes
Reliability59X applies
Replace the Monocross linkage needle roller bearings and plastic bushes with phosphor bronze bushes in stainless carriers. Self-lubricating, corrosion resistant, longer-lasting than OEM — especially valuable for off-road use where water and mud enter the linkage. Requires a machinist.
Effort:
YZ250 front end swap (43mm cartridge forks)
Performance
Pressing in a YZ250 front end gives vastly superior cartridge damping. Requires steering stem press, machining to fit YZ triple clamps to the XT600 headstock, and new wheel bearings. Best for primarily off-road use — for road use, progressive springs + 15W oil are more practical.

Stainless steel braided brake line (front)
PerformanceReliabilityEasy win
Eliminates hose expansion under pressure — lever feel becomes immediate and consistent. The 35-year-old rubber hose is also a safety concern regardless of feel. Goodridge, HEL, or Venhill — custom lengths to order. Fits stock banjo fittings on the TT/XT600 caliper.~€30–45

Rubber protector on brake/gear levers
ReliabilityEasy win
A strip of thick rubber sheet, holed and cable-tied to the inside edge of both levers. In a tip-over the rubber cushions the lever against the engine case instead of bending it inward and cracking the casing. Free from an offcut.

NGK CR8EIX iridium spark plug + NGK SD05F waterproof cap

The iridium electrode fires more reliably at lower voltages, improves cold-start, and lasts significantly longer. Particularly valuable after a 12V conversion. The NGK SD05F "jetski" plug cap has a more aggressive moisture seal for off-road/wet-weather use.

USB power socket at the headstock
Easy win
With the 12V conversion done, a switched waterproof USB module at the headstock feeds from the ignition-switched fuse slot. Powers GPS/phone. Oxford, Tecmate, or generic IP65 unit. The 59X headstock area is roomy with no fairing to obstruct.~€10–20

Double inner tube method (off-road tyres)
Reliability
Cut the old tube, remove the valve, slide it over the new tube before fitting inside the tyre. Doubles wall thickness at the bead — the most common site for pinch flats. Zero cost, 15 minutes extra on tyre fitting. Best used with a heavy-duty inner tube (Michelin Airstop) as the inner layer.

Wider footpeg platforms (DIY welded flat bar)
PerformanceEasy win
Weld flat bar around the existing pegs, notch with a grinder for grip. Adds ~30mm width and significantly more grip surface. ~€10–20 in materials. Alternatively, aftermarket wide rubber-covered pegs for the XT600 family bolt to stock mounts.
Effort:
SW-Motech centre stand
Easy win
The 59X has no centre stand from factory. SW-Motech and German aftermarket suppliers have made bolt-on units for the XT600/TT600 family. Invaluable for roadside maintenance. Verify compatibility with the 59X frame specifically before ordering.

Seat foam replacement (wider, medium density)
Performance
Cut the original foam, fit wider medium-density foam (not softer — softer bottoms out), optionally wrap with a sheepskin layer, refit the original cover. A local tapissier in France can do this for €40–60 including recovering. Significantly more comfortable on longer rides.

Effort:
Flywheel key rotation — manual timing advance
Performance
Rotating the flywheel key slightly advances the ignition base timing by 1–2° beyond the CDI's programmed curve. The Transmic FAQ's flat-track user (Dan) uses this on a high-compression 10:1 build. Requires removing the flywheel, filing the key slot by a precise amount, and verifying with a timing light. A mild 1° advance is safe on stock compression. More than that risks detonation unless paired with higher-octane fuel or a head skim.

Head port and polish
Performance
Port and polish of intake and exhaust runners — smooth casting flash, blend valve seats into ports, match intake manifold bore to carb throat. Do not mirror-polish the intake (slightly rough surface improves fuel atomisation). Exhaust ports can be polished. Best done by a specialist atelier moteur with experience on 4-valve single-cylinder heads.€80–200 at a French atelier depending on scope
Effort:
KEDO Devil Pipe header + SuperTrapp adjustable silencer
Performance
Most frequently cited exhaust upgrade in the tenere.co.uk forum. The SuperTrapp adjustable disc silencer lets you tune back pressure — more discs = more top-end flow, fewer = more mid-range. Without re-jetting after fitting a free-flowing exhaust you run lean and risk exhaust valve damage. Combine with a 145 main jet (up from stock ~132–135) and raised needle clip. Note for France: the SuperTrapp is loud in an open configuration — run fewer discs or a baffle insert for contrôle technique.

Exhaust header thermal wrap
ReliabilityEasy win
Wrapping the header in high-temperature fibreglass/ceramic tape keeps exhaust gas hotter and improves scavenging. Significantly reduces under-tank heat on this air-cooled single — relevant in slow French summer traffic. Soak the tape in water before wrapping — it conforms better and hardens as it dries. The Motomoritz philosophy: on a remote long-distance trip on a classic single-cylinder bike, a broken cable or electrical fault stops you dead — not for hours, but potentially for days. The solution is not just carrying spares, but pre-routing key spares on the bike so that in the event of failure, the repair is a 5-minute job rather than 2-hour roadside surgery.

Spare clutch cable — pre-routed parallel to the live one
Pre-routed
The core Motomoritz trick. A second clutch cable routed along the exact same path as the working cable, with both ends plugged. In the event the live cable snaps, unplug the spare, swap the barrel ends at lever and adjuster, and ride on in under 10 minutes — no threading a new cable through the frame from scratch.
How to pre-route
1. Order a second OEM clutch cable (same part as the live one).
2. Route through exactly the same guides and frame clips as the live cable.
3. Leave both ends free but plugged — do NOT connect at either end.
4. Zip-tie alongside the live cable every 15–20cm.
5. Mark with red tape at both ends to identify the spare.
Time to install:
Spare throttle cable — pre-routed
Pre-routed
Same principle. A broken throttle cable is an immediate stop with no workaround. Pre-route alongside the live cable from the perch, along the bars, down to the carb slide. Cap both ends. Check routing with the tank on — the tank can pinch cables if the path is poorly chosen.

Speedo cable + choke cable
Spare carried
Speedo cable not critical for riding but required for road legality in France. Choke cable breaks stuck-open = rich-running hot engine that won't restart. Both coil flat and weigh almost nothing in a tool bag. Source from XT600 43F/2KF listings.
Pre-wired on the bike
Shadow loom — pre-routed ignition-critical wires
Pre-routed
A simplified second loom covering only the critical ignition circuits, routed in a separate conduit alongside the main loom with Superseal connectors capped at each end. Since you're building a custom loom from scratch, this costs almost nothing extra in materials.
Minimum shadow circuit for the 59X (5 wires)
— Stator trigger → CDI (W/R wire)
— CDI +12V supply (R/W wire)
— CDI → Coil primary negative (B/Y wire)
— Kill switch ground (B/W wire)
— Common earth return (B wire)

In a crisis: unplug main loom connectors, plug in shadow loom, check for spark.

Spare fuses — full set of all ratings
Spare carried
Carry 3× each of every rating in your fuse block (5A, 10A, 20A). Store in a waterproof micro-container inside the fuse block cover. A blown fuse is a 30-second fix with spares; without them it's a crisis.
Spare spark plug + correct cap, pre-gapped
Spare carried
NGK CR8EIX gapped to 0.7–0.8mm in its original tube. Include the correct 16mm thin-wall plug socket — without the right socket you can't remove the plug when you need to.
Electrical diagnostic kit
Diagnostic
Compact multimeter (Uni-T UT61B or similar, ~€20, ~150g). Lets you diagnose any electrical fault: charging voltage, coil resistance, fuse continuity, earth integrity. Without it, electrical diagnosis is guesswork. Also carry: 30cm test lead with croc clips, contact cleaner, small Tesa tape roll, 4× Superseal connectors of each size used in your loom.
Full carburettor rebuild kit
Spare carried
Needle, main jet, pilot jet, float valve, all O-rings, diaphragm. If the carb floods or blocks in the middle of nowhere, you can rebuild it roadside in an hour. Full kit weighs under 50g. Source from XT600 listing — same carb family.
Petcock rebuild kit + spare inline fuel filter
Spare carried
A failed petcock diaphragm dumps fuel into the oil or floods the carb. A spare diaphragm kit is €5 and tiny. Also carry a spare inline fuel filter — 30 seconds to swap if the current one clogs with tank debris far from civilisation.
Cam chain tensioner (complete)
Spare carried
A worn tensioner giving out in the field = rattling engine you dare not rev. The complete unit (blade + bolt + spring) weighs almost nothing and fits in any tool bag. Source from XT600 43F/2KF listing.
1 litre of engine oil — carried on the bike
Spare carried
The 59X dry sump holds ~2 litres. Carry 1 litre in a flat flask secured to the frame. In remote areas the correct motorcycle oil grade may simply not be available. Off-road riding also accelerates oil consumption through blow-by.
Chain master link + 10 spare chain links
Spare carried
A broken master link leaves you pushing. Carry a clip-type master link for 520 chain and a 10-link spare section. Requires a chain breaker in your toolkit — check yours is actually there before leaving.
JB Weld + Devcon epoxy (emergency case repair)
Spare carried
A snapped chain or rocky drop can crack an engine case. JB Weld can seal a crack long enough to reach a workshop — not a real repair, but it buys 200–300km. 2 sachets, ~30g. Motomoritz-level prep, but it has saved riders in remote areas.
Minimum tool roll for the 59X
On bike
Spanners: 8, 10, 12, 14, 17mm combination. The 59X uses 10mm for most engine covers, 14mm for axles, 17mm for the front axle nut.

Hex keys: 4, 5, 6mm (Allen).

JIS screwdrivers: #2 and #3 — not Phillips. The 59X has JIS screws throughout.

Socket set: 16mm plug socket, 30mm for flywheel nut.

Pliers: combination + needle nose.

Chain breaker + rivet tool.

Tyre levers (3×) + bead buddy + tyre pressure gauge. The 59X runs tube tyres.

Tube tyre repair kit
Spare carried
Tube patches + vulcanising solution, CO2 inflator + spare cartridges (or a mini 12V compressor), tyre levers, tyre spoon. Plug kits (for tubeless) don't work here — you need actual patches or a spare tube. Carry one spare tube per wheel: 21" front, 18" rear. Pre-check valve integrity on spare tubes before the trip.
Handguards — Bark Busters or similar
On bike
A dropped bike in slow off-road riding snaps brake levers without handguards. Full handguards protect both lever and hand. Standard 22mm bar fitment on the 59X. Also protects the mirror mount and bar-end on a tip-over. Essential for any off-road use.
Spare brake lever + spare clutch lever
Spare carried
Even with handguards, levers snap in a serious fall. The 59X lever pivots are shared with XT600. Carry one spare of each. Flat, ~50g each, barely noticeable in a pannier.
Auxiliary fuel — 1–2L flat flask
Spare carried
The 59X plastic tank holds 13L — decent range (~250–350km depending on pace) but in remote areas service stations can be 200km apart. A 1L flat fuel flask (MSR or Touratech) strapped to the frame or inside a pannier buys 30–40km emergency range. More importantly it removes fuel anxiety on long sections.
